[[dpp]]
= Digital Product Passport (DPP)

== Introduction

To support the implementation of digital product passports (DPPs), 8 standards have been developed in CEN CENELEC:

* EN 18216:2026 – Digital product passport – Data exchange protocols
* EN 18219:2026 – Digital product passport – Unique identifiers
* EN 18220:2026 – Digital product passport – Data carriers
* EN 18221:2026 – Digital product passport – Data storage, archiving, and data persistence
* EN 18222:2026 – Digital Product Passport – Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) for the product passport lifecycle management and searchability
* EN 18223:2026 – Digital Product Passport – System interoperability
* EN 18239:2026 – Digital Product Passport – Access rights management, information system security, and business confidentiality
* EN 18246:2026 – Digital Product Passport – Data authentication, reliability and integrity

This informative annex describes how an Asset Administration Shell (AAS) system can support DPP access control, data authenticity, change history, and recovery.
It relates these concerns to existing AAS capabilities and proposes integration measures for DPP services.
The access rules and workflows are examples. The HTTP response-signing approach is a proposal in this annex, not an established AAS API convention or a requirement attributed to EN 18239 or EN 18246.
Conformity with the referenced standards requires a separate assessment.

== Regulatory and specification context

Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 establishes the DPP framework xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib17[[17\]].
It requires data authentication, reliability and integrity, a high level of security and privacy, protection against fraud, controlled rights to modify DPP data, a backup copy, and continued DPP availability for the period specified by applicable delegated acts.
The delegated act for a product group determines the concrete DPP information, stakeholder access rights, granularity, and availability period; that period corresponds at least to the expected lifetime of the product.

The following European DPP standards are particularly relevant to this security mapping:

* EN 18239 covers access rights management, information system security, data protection, business confidentiality, and responsibility transfer xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib13[[13\]];
* EN 18246 covers data authentication, reliability, integrity, and secure electronically signed data constructs xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib14[[14\]];
* EN 18221 covers decentralized DPP storage, archiving, historical data, persistence, replication, and data-lifetime definition xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib15[[15\]]; and
* EN 18222 covers DPP lifecycle operations, including historical retrieval xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib16[[16\]].


The mappings are informative and do not establish clause-by-clause conformity with these standards.

Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), applies in parallel to products with digital elements within its scope xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib24[[24\]].
Its essential cybersecurity requirements address, among other matters, integrity of stored and processed data, recording and monitoring of access to or modification of data and functions, availability after an incident, vulnerability handling, and documentation of software components through a software bill of materials.
These requirements can also apply to DPP data and supporting products or services within the CRA's scope.
The CRA does not define a DPP history format, DPP evidence storage, or a universal DPP retention period.
Its 24-hour period concerns an early warning notification for an actively exploited vulnerability or severe incident; it is not a deadline for retrieval of a historical DPP or its evidence.

The EU DPP Registry, identity and trust mechanisms, identifier and data-carrier systems, semantic repositories, and DPP service providers belong to the wider DPP environment.
Unique identifiers, data carriers, and system interoperability are addressed by EN 18219, EN 18220, and EN 18223 respectively xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib21[[21\]] xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib22[[22\]] xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib23[[23\]].
Registry evidence can demonstrate registration properties of a specified DPP, actor, and time xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib18[[18\]].
Electronic identification, signatures, seals, and trust services can supply identity and trust context xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib19[[19\]] xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib20[[20\]].
Neither registration nor cryptographic verification by itself establishes the factual correctness or legal compliance of every product statement.

=== Authentication and AAS authorization

The protocols for authenticating a requester and issuing an access token are outside this annex. A requester can be a person or a client application. The DPP IAM can authenticate that requester or accept an identity from a trusted source, for example an EU Digital Identity Wallet, and issue a token with claims used by the AAS. This annex does not define the wallet protocol, identity federation, or claim mapping.

The AAS interface validates the access token and its trusted claims before using them to evaluate Access Rules; see xref:../access-rule-model.adoc#access-tokens[Access Tokens]. This authenticates the identity attributes used for access control. It does not establish the authenticity of the returned DPP data, which is addressed separately in <<dpp-response-signing>>.

In the illustrated access model, the AAS Access Rule Model performs authorization. The DPP service resolves the product identifier and maps the request to AAS API calls without a separate authorization decision. For a request made on behalf of another actor, the AAS needs claims and resource scopes that express that actor's authority. A provider token with broader permissions does not preserve the original requester's restrictions by itself. A public AAS call that relies on `GLOBAL(ANONYMOUS)` is made without an access token.

=== Illustrative Battery Passport access rules

The following examples use the AAS Access Rule Model to grant access to Battery Passport Submodels or Submodel-repository routes. Applicable legislation determines which actors can read or modify which data; these examples do not define a general Battery Passport policy.

The examples use the primary Submodel `semanticId` values defined in the following published Battery Passport Submodel Templates:

* link:https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/idta-submodel-templates/IDTA-02035-3/1.0/index.html#_properties_of_the_submodel_carbonfootprint[IDTA-02035-3 Product Carbon Footprint, version 1.0].
* link:https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/idta-submodel-templates/IDTA-02035-4/1.0.1/index.html#_submodel_technicaldata[IDTA-02035-4 Technical Data, version 1.0.1].
* link:https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/idta-submodel-templates/IDTA-02035-5/1.0.2/index.html#_properties_of_the_submodel_productcondition[IDTA-02035-5 Product Condition, version 1.0.2].
* link:https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/idta-submodel-templates/IDTA-02035-7/1.0.1/index.html#_properties_of_the_submodel_circularity[IDTA-02035-7 Circularity, version 1.0.1].

The rules compare the exact semantic identifiers; they do not retrieve them as web pages. The links above lead to their definitions. A template's document version can differ from the version in its semantic identifier: Circularity version 1.0.1 retains `urn:samm:io.admin-shell.idta.batterypass.circularity:1.0.0#Circularity`. Deployments using other template versions need to check the corresponding identifiers before applying these rules.

`IDENTIFIABLE $sm("*")` selects candidate Submodels without relying on their instance identifiers or `idShort` paths; `$sm#semanticId` then restricts the rule to instances of the intended templates. The templates define data semantics, not regulatory access classes. A deployment therefore verifies the illustrative grouping against the applicable access-rights specification. If different access classes coexist within one Submodel, the deployment defines finer `REFERABLE` or filter rules for the affected SubmodelElements instead of granting the entire Submodel.

The examples below separate the access policy for a requester from the deployment used to host the DPP and AAS. Examples 1 to 4 illustrate stakeholder access. Examples 5 to 8 illustrate service-provider and economic-operator access in three alternative deployments. Select the rules needed for the deployment; these examples are not a policy to install unchanged.

==== Shared object definitions and rule structure

The rule blocks refer to the following object groups with `USEOBJECTS`. Include the required definitions when assembling a policy from the examples.
`BatteryPassport-Submodels` selects candidate Submodels for examples 1 to 6; each rule's `FORMULA` restricts them by `semanticId`.
`Submodel-Repository-Routes` selects the repository routes used in examples 7 and 8.

[source,bnf]
----
DEFOBJECTS "BatteryPassport-Submodels"
  IDENTIFIABLE $sm("*")

DEFOBJECTS "Submodel-Repository-Routes"
  ROUTE "/submodels"
  ROUTE "/submodels/*"
----

In each rule, `ATTRIBUTES` lists the inputs used for access control, `RIGHTS` lists the permitted operations, and `OBJECTS` selects the targets. `FORMULA` states the conditions under which `ACCESS: ALLOW` applies. Claims come from a validated access token. `GLOBAL(ANONYMOUS)` instead applies to calls without a token. An operation remains denied unless an applicable rule permits it.

The standard route literals are `/submodels` and `/submodels/*`. A deployment-specific tenant prefix belongs to the server base URL and is not part of these literals. The route definitions alone do not isolate tenants; scenario B explains the required partition selection.

[[dpp-rule-public-read]]
==== Example 1: public user reads public Battery Passport data

A public user requests a DPP by product ID. The DPP service calls the AAS without an access token, as shown in <<image-dpp-public-read>>.
This rule permits `READ` only for Technical Data and Product Carbon Footprint Submodels. It grants no write permission or anonymous access to `GetAllSubmodels`; the DPP service reads addressed Submodels that match the allowed semantics.

[source,bnf]
----
ACCESSRULE:
  ATTRIBUTES:
    GLOBAL(ANONYMOUS)
  RIGHTS: READ
  ACCESS: ALLOW
  OBJECTS:
    USEOBJECTS "BatteryPassport-Submodels"
  FORMULA:
    $or(
      $sm#semanticId $eq "https://admin-shell.io/idta/digitalbatterypassport/TechnicalData/1/0",
      $sm#semanticId $eq "https://admin-shell.io/idta/CarbonFootprint/CarbonFootprint/1/0"
    )
----

[[dpp-rule-recycler-read]]
==== Example 2: recycler reads data needed for recycling

An authenticated recycler requests the Battery Passport. The token contains `Role = "recycler"`.
This rule permits `READ` on Technical Data, Product Carbon Footprint, Product Condition, and Circularity Submodels. It does not grant update permission. The AAS authorization step in <<image-dpp-authenticated-read>> applies this rule when the requester is a recycler.

[source,bnf]
----
ACCESSRULE:
  ATTRIBUTES:
    CLAIM("Role")
  RIGHTS: READ
  ACCESS: ALLOW
  OBJECTS:
    USEOBJECTS "BatteryPassport-Submodels"
  FORMULA:
    $and(
      CLAIM("Role") $eq "recycler",
      $or(
        $sm#semanticId $eq "https://admin-shell.io/idta/digitalbatterypassport/TechnicalData/1/0",
        $sm#semanticId $eq "https://admin-shell.io/idta/CarbonFootprint/CarbonFootprint/1/0",
        $sm#semanticId $eq "urn:samm:io.admin-shell.idta.batterypass.product_condition:1.0.2#ProductCondition",
        $sm#semanticId $eq "urn:samm:io.admin-shell.idta.batterypass.circularity:1.0.0#Circularity"
      )
    )
----

[[dpp-rule-recycler-update]]
==== Example 3: delegated recycler updates Product Condition

A recycler records a change to the battery's condition. The economic operator remains responsible for the Battery Passport information and can authorize another operator to act on its behalf.
This rule requires both `Role = "recycler"` and `DppUpdateAuthorized = "true"`. It permits `UPDATE` only on the Product Condition Submodel, as shown in <<image-dpp-recycler-update>>. Read access is granted separately by <<dpp-rule-recycler-read,example 2>>.

The permission flag and semantic identifier do not identify the products covered by the delegation. A deployment also needs to restrict the selected objects to those products; the recycler role alone is insufficient.

[source,bnf]
----
ACCESSRULE:
  ATTRIBUTES:
    CLAIM("Role")
    CLAIM("DppUpdateAuthorized")
  RIGHTS: UPDATE
  ACCESS: ALLOW
  OBJECTS:
    USEOBJECTS "BatteryPassport-Submodels"
  FORMULA:
    $and(
      CLAIM("Role") $eq "recycler",
      CLAIM("DppUpdateAuthorized") $eq "true",
      $sm#semanticId $eq "urn:samm:io.admin-shell.idta.batterypass.product_condition:1.0.2#ProductCondition"
    )
----

[[dpp-rule-tenant-operator]]
==== Example 7: economic operator manages data in a hosted tenant partition (scenario B)

A DPP service provider hosts both the DPP service and the AAS for several economic operators. An operator creates, reads, or updates its own data through the provider's DPP service, as shown in <<image-dpp-economic-operator-update>>.
This rule requires `Role = "tenant-economic-operator"` and `tenant = "example-tenant"`. It permits `CREATE`, `READ`, and `UPDATE` on the configured Submodel-repository routes; it grants no `DELETE` permission.

Before evaluating the route rule, the AAS maps the validated tenant claim to a trusted tenant-specific partition or object scope. Merely checking the claim does not provide isolation. The example is instantiated for the configured tenant and applies only within that partition.

[source,bnf]
----
ACCESSRULE:
  ATTRIBUTES:
    CLAIM("Role")
    CLAIM("tenant")
  RIGHTS: CREATE READ UPDATE
  ACCESS: ALLOW
  OBJECTS:
    USEOBJECTS "Submodel-Repository-Routes"
  FORMULA:
    $and(
      CLAIM("Role") $eq "tenant-economic-operator",
      CLAIM("tenant") $eq "example-tenant"
    )
----

Blue denotes AAS capabilities; yellow denotes DPP-specific integration responsibilities.
Gray denotes external legislation, DPP requirements, registration, identity, semantics, and trust information.
An implementation can combine or distribute these capabilities while preserving their security relationships.

DPP-specific responsibilities can be implemented in the DPP service or in supporting components.

The blue boxes separate the access-control checks from the resources and operations they protect:

* *AAS protection* validates access tokens and trusted claims, then evaluates Access Rules for the requested operation and objects. Public requests are evaluated against the applicable anonymous-access rules.
* *AAS resources and services* provide the protected read and write operations, for example reading a Submodel or updating a Product Condition property. The AAS interface enforces the access decision before returning protected data or applying a change.

The arrows show dependencies: the DPP context supplies identity and trust information; the IAM supplies tokens; DPP calls pass through AAS protection to reach AAS resources. These functions can be implemented within the same service.

The DPP service resolves a product identifier, calls the AAS services, and composes the returned data into a DPP. The link:https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/aas-specifications/IDTA-01002/v3.2/annex/dpp.html[Part 2 DPP annex] describes the mapping of DPP operations from EN 18222 to AAS services. The HTTP response-signing proposal below is defined here, separately from that mapping.

The DPP service can associate changes with audit records, provide signatures for response verification, and coordinate recovery from retained versions and backups. The applicable DPP requirements determine which records and versions need to be retained. This annex does not define an evidence format, retention period, or recovery procedure.

[[dpp-response-signing]]
=== Proposed HTTP response signing (informative)

This proposal uses HTTP Message Signatures (RFC 9421) and Content-Digest (RFC 9530) to let a requester verify the origin and integrity of a native DPP read response xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib25[[25\]] xref:../bibliography.adoc#bib26[[26\]]. It describes one implementation approach for a DPP service. It does not establish conformity with EN 18246 or replace its requirements for electronically signed data constructs.

The requester asks the DPP service for data. The DPP service retrieves the authorized data from AAS services, composes the response, and acts as its signer. Public users and authenticated requesters can both request a signed response. The signature identifies the DPP service as signer; it does not by itself prove that the original data publisher signed the data or that the product statements are correct.

In this proposal, the requester opts in with `Accept-Signature`. Without that field, the DPP service returns an unsigned read response. This opt-in behavior is a choice of the proposal; RFC 9421 also permits unsolicited signatures.

For an accepted signature request, the DPP service computes `Content-Digest` over the returned content. It includes that field, the response status and content type, and the originating request's method and target URI in the signature coverage. Request components use the RFC 9421 `;req` parameter. `Signature-Input` identifies the covered components and signature parameters; `Signature` carries the signature value. The presence of these fields alone is not sufficient: the requester verifies both the signature with a trusted key and the digest against the received content.

An interoperable profile also needs to specify supported algorithms, signer-key discovery and trust, freshness and replay checks, cache handling, and the response to an unsupported signature request. These choices remain open in this proposal; the workflow diagrams show successful requests only.

For later verification, a requester can retain the response content, the signature fields, all covered request and response components, and the necessary key and trust evidence. This choice does not replace any retention duties of the economic operator or service provider. A signature on a historical read response establishes what the service returned at retrieval time; it does not by itself prove that the historical record has remained unchanged.

The proposal uses the normal DPP read endpoints, without adding `/$sign` or `/$signed` DPP endpoints. The AAS `/$signed` endpoints described in link:https://industrialdigitaltwin.io/aas-specifications/IDTA-01002/v3.2/signatures.html[Part 2 Signatures] return signed AAS representations; they do not sign the composed DPP response.
